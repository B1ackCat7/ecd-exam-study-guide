## A

**Activity:** Work performed by a program, such as business visits or workshops.

**Additionality:** The part of an outcome that likely would not have occurred, or would have occurred later or at smaller scale, without the intervention.

**Aftercare:** Structured relationship and support for firms already established in a community, often to encourage retention and reinvestment.

**Arm's-length organization:** A separate corporation or agency that delivers a public mandate with defined independence and accountability.

**Assets:** Present economic resources controlled by an entity as a result of past events. Under IFRS, an economic resource is a right with the potential to produce economic benefits.

## B

**Balance sheet:** A statement of financial position presenting assets, liabilities, and equity at a specified date.

**Benchmark:** A baseline, target, historical result, or comparable entity used for interpretation.

**BR&E:** Business retention and expansion; a relationship, intelligence, and action process focused on existing firms.

**Business attraction:** Effort to identify and secure compatible new investment or business activity.

**Business climate:** The regulatory, cost, infrastructure, service, relationship, and market environment experienced by firms.

## C

**Capital budget:** Plan for acquisition and financing of long-lived assets.

**Cash flow:** Inflows and outflows of cash and cash equivalents during a period, commonly classified as operating, investing, or financing.

**Cluster:** Interconnected firms, suppliers, institutions, workers, and supporting organizations in a related field and geography.

**Community profile:** Current, sourced information used by firms and investors to assess a location.

**Competitive analysis:** Quantitative and qualitative assessment of the community's economic position, assets, performance, and opportunities.

**Conflict of interest:** A situation in which a private interest, relationship, or competing duty impairs, may impair, or could reasonably appear to impair objective professional judgment.

**Conversion rate:** Proportion of cases that move from one defined stage to the next.

**Cost-benefit analysis:** Comparison of social benefits and costs of alternatives, generally across a defined time horizon.

**Current ratio:** Current assets divided by current liabilities.

## D

**Days sales outstanding:** Estimated average collection period, normally calculated using average accounts receivable divided by average daily net credit sales.

**Deadweight:** Benefits that likely would have occurred without the intervention.

**Debt-to-equity:** A leverage ratio comparing a defined debt measure with a defined equity measure. Sources variously use interest-bearing debt or total liabilities, so state the convention.

**Development impact analysis:** Assessment of fiscal, environmental, socio-economic, transportation, and other consequences of development.

**Differential shift:** The shift-share component associated with local industry performance relative to the same industry in the reference area; also called the local, regional, or competitive effect.

**Direct effect:** Initial activity generated by the project or organization being analyzed.

**Displacement:** Activity that replaces or moves existing activity rather than creating net new benefit.

**Diversification:** Reduced dependence on a narrow set of sectors, firms, customers, markets, or resources.

## E

**Economic base:** Industries and activities that bring income into a region by selling to markets outside it or attracting external spending. Economic-base analysis contrasts these with activity serving primarily local demand.

**Economic impact:** A change in measures such as employment, labour income, output, or value added associated with an activity or intervention. Scope, geography, time, counterfactual, and direct, indirect, and induced effects should be stated.

**Economic resilience:** Capacity to anticipate, withstand, adapt to, and recover from shocks.

**Equity:** In accounting, the residual interest in assets after deducting liabilities. In community analysis, fairness that recognizes different circumstances and seeks substantively fair access, process, and distribution of effects. State the intended meaning.

**Evaluation:** Systematic assessment of program design, delivery, results, and value.

## F

**FDI:** Foreign direct investment; cross-border investment associated with lasting interest and influence in an enterprise.

**Fiscal impact:** Effect on public revenue, cost, capital, and financial position.

## G

**Governance:** Structures and processes for direction, authority, accountability, risk, and oversight.

**Gross margin:** Sales less cost of goods sold, divided by sales.

## I

**Impact:** A significant positive or negative, intended or unintended, higher-level effect to which an intervention contributes. Evaluation frameworks differ on whether the term is reserved for longer-term change.

**Income statement:** Statement of revenue, expenses, and profit over a period.

**Indirect effect:** Activity generated in suppliers by the direct activity.

**Induced effect:** Activity associated with spending of labour income from direct and indirect activity.

**Industrial mix effect:** Shift-share component associated with the reference industry's growth relative to overall reference growth.

**Input:** Resource used by a program, such as staff, money, data, or facilities.

**Interest coverage:** An earnings measure divided by interest expense; EBIT divided by interest expense is common, but definitions vary.

**Inventory turnover:** Cost of goods sold divided by average inventory.

## L

**Land-use bylaw or zoning bylaw:** Regulation that establishes land uses and development standards; terminology varies by jurisdiction.

**Lead:** A possible opportunity requiring validation.

**Leakage:** Benefits that flow outside the intended area or population.

**Liabilities:** Present obligations to transfer an economic resource as a result of past events.

**Liquidity:** Ability to meet near-term obligations.

**Location quotient:** Ratio comparing an industry's share of a regional measure with the same industry's share of that measure in a reference economy. Employment is common, but earnings, output, or GDP may also be used.

**Logic model:** Connection among inputs, activities, outputs, and outcomes.

## M

**Market-to-book:** Market price per share divided by book value per share.

**Marketing:** Targeting, positioning, communication, and lead-generation activity.

**Mentoring:** Developmental relationship in which experience supports another person's capability and judgment.

## N

**NAICS:** North American Industry Classification System.

**Net margin:** Net income divided by sales.

## O

**Official plan or municipal plan:** A statutory or policy plan setting long-term land-use and growth direction. Its name, required content, approval process, and legal effect vary by jurisdiction.

**Operating budget:** Plan for recurring revenues and expenditures.

**Outcome:** A short- or medium-term change in condition, behaviour, capacity, or performance that an intervention is expected to influence through its activities and outputs.

**Output:** A product, capital good, or service delivered by an intervention, such as firms served, workshops delivered, or reports completed.

## P

**P3:** Public-private partnership; an arrangement allocating project functions and risks between public and private parties.

**Performance measure:** Indicator used to monitor implementation, efficiency, output, outcome, or impact.

**Price-earnings ratio:** Market price per share divided by earnings per share.

**Project:** In this guide's investment funnel, a qualified and actively managed investment opportunity. Pipeline terminology varies among organizations and customer-relationship systems.

**Prospect:** A lead judged sufficiently suitable for active follow-up. Sales and investment-pipeline terminology varies by organization.

## Q

**Qualified lead/project:** A verified opportunity meeting the organization's documented criteria, normally including fit, need, decision authority, resources, and timing.

**Quick ratio:** Quick assets divided by current liabilities. Quick assets commonly include cash, short-term investments, and receivables; simplified formulas may subtract inventory and less-liquid prepaids from current assets.

## R

**Reference-area growth effect:** Shift-share component associated with overall employment growth in the reference economy.

**Return on assets:** Net income divided by average total assets.

**Return on equity:** Net income available to common shareholders divided by average common equity.

**Rights-holder:** A person or collective holding legal, constitutional, treaty, inherent, or other recognized rights relevant to a decision. In the Crown's duty-to-consult context, the term refers to Indigenous rights-holding collectives, not merely interested parties.

## S

**Sales:** Relationship and decision work that moves a qualified project toward commitment.

**Sector strategy:** Coordinated plan to strengthen a defined industry or economic field.

**Shift-share:** Method decomposing local employment change into reference growth, industry mix, and local shift.

**Site selection:** Business decision process used to compare, eliminate, and select locations.

**SMART:** Specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound. Variants use words such as realistic, results-focused, or audience-specific; define the version being used.

**Stakeholder:** A person, group, or organization affected by, interested in, or able to influence a decision. Do not use the term as a substitute for Indigenous rights-holder.

**Strategic plan:** Evidence-based framework that links mission, priorities, actions, resources, and measurement.

**SWOT:** Strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats; usually strengths and weaknesses are internal, while opportunities and threats arise externally.

## T

**Target sector:** Industry or business function selected for focused development based on evidence and fit.

**Theory of change:** Explanation of how and why activities are expected to create outcomes.

**Triple bottom line:** A framework that considers economic, social, and environmental performance or value rather than financial performance alone.

## V

**Value proposition:** Evidence-based explanation of why a target should choose or engage with a community.

## W

**Working capital:** Current assets minus current liabilities.
