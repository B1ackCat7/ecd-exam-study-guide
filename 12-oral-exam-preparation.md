## What to know about your local economy

Prepare current, sourced knowledge of:

- geography and regional relationships;
- population, growth, age, migration, and households;
- labour force, participation, unemployment, occupations, skills, wages, and commuting;
- leading sectors and major employers using only public information;
- business size and entrepreneurship;
- exports, supply chains, and market exposure;
- land, buildings, infrastructure, utilities, broadband, transportation, ports, and airports;
- housing, health, education, and training;
- municipal fiscal and development context;
- strategic priorities and active public initiatives;
- Indigenous governments, communities, rights, and relationships;
- climate and resilience risks;
- opportunities, constraints, and competitors;
- recent economic events.

For every number, know the source year. If multiple sources differ, know why.

Do not place confidential employer or client information on this sheet.

## Answer structures

### Direct factual question

> Answer -> source/date -> significance -> limitation

Example pattern:

> "The latest public estimate is X as of Y. I use it because... The main limitation is... I would supplement it with..."

### Scenario question

> Objective -> facts -> stakeholders -> action -> measures -> risk

### Experience question

Use CARL:

- **Context**
- **Action**
- **Result**
- **Learning**

Be clear about your own role. "We" can hide whether you led, supported, or observed.

### Recommendation question

> Recommendation -> reasons -> implementation -> measure -> contingency

State the recommendation early. Do not make the proctor infer it.

## Original practice questions

1. Describe your local economy in two minutes.
2. What are its three most important economic opportunities and why?
3. What is its most serious vulnerability?
4. How do global events reach your local businesses?
5. How would you know whether the economy is becoming more diversified?
6. Describe a project in which you built consensus among groups with different interests.
7. Describe a time evidence changed your recommendation.
8. How do you protect confidentiality while maintaining public accountability?
9. What is the EDO's role in a controversial development proposal?
10. What measures best demonstrate the value of your economic development work?
11. How would you respond to a major employer closure?
12. How would you improve the local business climate without using incentives?
13. What should be in an investment-ready community profile?
14. Explain a location quotient to a councillor without jargon.
15. What financial warning signs would cause you to refer a business to a qualified advisor?
16. How should an EDO work with Indigenous rights-holders and economic development partners?
17. What is one professional mistake or weak result from which you learned?
18. What current issue will matter most to your community over the next three years?

## Delivery

- Aim for 60 to 120 seconds unless the question clearly calls for depth.
- Signpost: "There are three parts..."
- Use concrete verbs and short sentences.
- Avoid jargon, filler, and unsupported superlatives.
- Distinguish facts from judgment.
- Pause instead of filling silence.
- If a question is unclear, ask for clarification.
- If you do not know, say what you know, what is missing, and how you would find it.
- Never invent data.

## Challenging follow-ups

Practise:

- What evidence supports that?
- Who disagreed and why?
- What authority did you have?
- What did the project cost?
- What would have happened without your action?
- How did you protect confidentiality?
- Who benefited and who bore the cost?
- What did you measure?
- What would you do differently?

## Mock oral process

1. Ask a colleague to select nine prompts without advance notice.
2. Record a 30-minute session.
3. Review for directness, structure, evidence, role clarity, and timing.
4. Transcribe only weak answers.
5. Reduce each weak answer to a five-line outline.
6. Repeat with new questions.

## Final oral checklist

- current local data refreshed;
- three strong and three difficult experience examples;
- current issue brief prepared;
- no confidential notes visible during screen share;
- quiet room, camera, microphone, power, and connection checked;
- water and identification ready;
- concise opening and closing habits practised.
